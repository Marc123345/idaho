How to read the territory

Rings are straight-line distance from Rigby. Mountain routing adds roughly 20 to 35 percent through the passes, so the 25-mile ring is home turf, the 65-mile ring is a comfortable same-day round trip, and the 100-mile edge is a planned day out. Bubble size is the count of businesses with eleven or more staff in that city, the simplest public signal for "occupies a real commercial building". Pins are named properties with flat or low-slope roofs, each tied to a published source.

    What the campaign targets

    RoofCoat campaigns geo-target this ring on Facebook and YouTube and speak to the people who own or manage these buildings: facility managers, property managers, owner-operators, school and hospital plant directors. Leads arrive pre-qualified for property type, urgency and budget, by text and email in real time.

    Why this ring works for coatings

    Eastern Idaho and Jackson Hole carry heavy snow load and hard freeze-thaw cycles, which is what opens seams and ponds water on flat membranes. The potato and dairy processing base adds hundreds of thousands of square feet of wet, steamy, refrigerated roof where a leak costs product. Both are strong restoration arguments.

    Season

    Coating season here runs roughly May to early October. A campaign that starts in late winter builds a spring backlog; one that starts now catches the fall push before the snow.

    How this was built. Business counts come from a commercial business database and are counted for the seven urban areas it tracks inside the ring; small towns show as hollow markers with population only. "11+ staff" is the count of businesses reporting eleven or more employees. Every named property was found in a published web source and carries a link to it; nothing was invented, and where no address or size was stated the field is left blank rather than guessed. Placement. Pins sit at the city center, not the street address, so a city with several prospects reads as one cluster. Open the city to see each one. Priority. A, B and C tiers weight building type against whatever size signal the source gave (square footage, building count, headcount). They rank the list; they are not appraisals, and roof age and condition were not verified from the ground. Square footage on record sums only the figures a source stated, so the real roof area in the ring is far larger.
